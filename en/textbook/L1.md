# Kairos Grade 1 Textbook — Operating Kairos

**Version**: v1.1 (2026-08-03 · conforms to language 1.0 〈tracked 2026-11-04〉 · spec_head: v1.1.0 — external review round 7 E1 handled: the covering interpretation in Chapter 1 aligned with the canonical source · a contrasting example of the boot practice added) — English mirror of the Japanese original. The aim of Grade 1 is to **design schedule definitions that do not betray you in production
operation** — beyond reading (Grade 3) and writing (Grade 2) lies the **semantics of operation**, including the freshness of data, the
reliability of results and the division of labor with the execution system. The [language specification](https://kairos-lang.org/en/spec/) is always canonical —
this book only shows the way to operate, and never redefines the specification.

**How to read this book**: understanding at Grade 1 **is complete with the text and the end-of-chapter exercises**. The reference links are for confirming against the canonical source (start from
examples with the [reference](https://kairos-lang.org/en/reference/)). Every "Run it and read" example in the text is **execution-verified**
(`# eval:` is the evaluation range · the block right after it is the actual output). The "▶ Open in the Playground" link under each example lets you run and modify it in the browser as is (no installation · [kairos-lang.org/en/playground](https://kairos-lang.org/en/playground/)). The protagonists of Grade 1 are the
**lines under** the firing points — the `# ⚠` annotations and `# 被覆サマリ` (the coverage summary). These few lines, skipped at Grades 3 and 2,
become the main text from this chapter on.

To reproduce with the CLI: `npx kairos-lang list --from a --to b --tz Asia/Tokyo definition.kairos` (put the a and b of `# eval: a..b`. Always pass `--tz` — the CLI defaults to the machine's time zone, and with a different one the dates come out as the previous day with a time).

## Chapter 1 Declaring the freshness of data — covering and the "legitimate empty"

Holidays, business days, solar terms — much of the material of a schedule cannot be computed and can only be **brought in as data**.
And data always has an edge: "I know only up to here". Kairos makes you **declare** this edge with `covering:` —
it is the claim "this sequence is complete for this range", and it **does not touch the values**
(it neither adds nor removes points within the range).

The power of the declaration shows most when the sequence is **empty**. The empty table `[] covering: range` is the primary form of "zero points, but I want to
claim coverage" (ADR-45) — and the reading of covering does not change when empty.
`[] covering: 2027..2027` is the claim "**I know 2027 completely, and there are zero holidays**":

**Run it and read** — an empty table plus a wide covering is a complete claim of "zero holidays":

```kairos
# eval: 2027-01-04..2027-01-11
premise JP {
  calendar-system: Gregorian
  tz: "Asia/Tokyo"
  wkst: Mon
}

@JP
holidays2027 = [] covering: 2027..2027
satSun = everyDay |> filter(d => weekday(d) == Sat or weekday(d) == Sun)
bizDay = everyDay \ (satSun | holidays2027)

bizDay
```

```text
2027-01-04
2027-01-05
2027-01-06
2027-01-07
2027-01-08
# 被覆サマリ
#   holidays2027 covering 2027-01-01..2027-12-31 残走路 355 日
```

The 5 points Mon–Fri fire without annotation, and the coverage summary reports a runway (残走路) of 355 days. No annotation appears because we are inside the claim —
**and that is also the danger of this form**. If the reality is "the 2027 holiday data has **not arrived yet**", this expression is an overclaim: the real holiday 2027-01-01 (New Year's Day) **fires as a business day without annotation**,
and the +355-day runway returns **false reassurance**. Coverage can speak even of what you do not know —
the wider you make it, the stronger the claim.

The correct way to write "not arrived yet" is the **minimal claim of the observation day only** — narrow the coverage to the
day you actually confirmed (the example takes the writing date 2026-08-02 as the observation day):

**Run it and read** — the same empty table, but with honest coverage the annotation and the negative runway say "put the data in":

```kairos
# eval: 2027-01-04..2027-01-11
premise JP {
  calendar-system: Gregorian
  tz: "Asia/Tokyo"
  wkst: Mon
}

@JP
holidays2027 = [] covering: 2026-08-02..2026-08-02
satSun = everyDay |> filter(d => weekday(d) == Sat or weekday(d) == Sun)
bizDay = everyDay \ (satSun | holidays2027)

bizDay
```

```text
2027-01-04
2027-01-05
2027-01-06
2027-01-07
2027-01-08
# ⚠ 範囲外 2027-01-04..2027-01-11（holidays2027 covering 2026-08-02..2026-08-02）
# 被覆サマリ
#   holidays2027 covering 2026-08-02..2026-08-02 残走路 -161 日
```

The point sequence itself is the same, but the **out-of-coverage annotation** (範囲外註釈) runs alongside saying "this result is degraded outside the holiday data", and
the runway is **immediately negative** — it says, machine-readably, not "before it runs out" but "it has already run out" (degrades but
**observably**, is the promise of Kairos). This is the boot practice for empty tables — even with zero points, the coverage is only the range you
know ([reference/table-literal](https://kairos-lang.org/en/reference/table-literal.html),
"Pitfalls"). Conversely, leaving the end of covering open (`covering: 2027-01-01..`) is the strongest claim, "from then on
this sequence is complete **forever**" (runway ∞), and the runway signal disappears permanently —
use it only for data that can truly claim completeness.

## Chapter 2 Telling empties apart — the three branches of effective coverage

When the result is **empty**, Kairos distinguishes "why it is empty" by the shape of the output. There are three faces:

1. **Materialization horizon** — you hit the edge of the computation range. Reported by the `horizon-clip` **warning** — a line of the form
   `警告: horizon-clip: shift 〈point〉（計算範囲 to+400日 の実体化地平線——言語の地平線ではない。ADR-37 判断 8）`
   is attached at the end of the output (〈point〉 is the clipped landing point · besides shift it also appears with roll / snapTo / takeLast and others. "Evaluation to + 400 days" is
   **a constant of the reference implementation**, not the language's horizon — a different implementation has a different width. Revisit the design of the evaluation range).
2. **Outside the effective coverage** (実効被覆域) — you stepped into a range the data "does not know". An **out-of-coverage annotation** (a `# ⚠` line)
   runs alongside (**unknown** — updating the supply might produce points).
3. **Legitimate exhaustion of complete data** — it is **settled** that there is nothing applicable. Nothing is attached; just empty
   (nothing needs to be done).

**Run it and read** — ② and ③ with the same "zero points". The only difference is the covering declaration (both print `# 0 点（…に該当なし）` on the first line
— zero points do not stay silent. What tells them apart is whether `# ⚠` annotation lines follow):

```kairos
# eval: 2027-01-01..2027-06-01
premise JP {
  calendar-system: Gregorian
  tz: "Asia/Tokyo"
  wkst: Mon
}

@JP
sekkiA = [2026-02-04, 2026-05-05] covering: 2026..2026
([2027-03-01] covering: ..) |> roll(Following, on: sekkiA)
```

```text
# 0 点（[2027-01-01, 2027-06-01) に該当なし）
# ⚠ 範囲外 2027-01-01..2027-06-01（sekkiA covering 2026-01-01..2026-12-31）
# 被覆サマリ
#   (無名テーブル) covering ..（完結主張） 残走路 ∞
#   sekkiA covering 2026-01-01..2026-12-31 残走路 -151 日
```

The operational branches correspond directly: for ① revisit the evaluation range · for ② **update the supply** · for ③ **do
nothing**. Neither "empty, therefore abnormal" nor "empty, therefore normal" — **the output tells you the kind of empty** — and being able to
feed this to monitoring while it is still machine-readable is the decisive difference from a system built with if statements and a holiday table.

## Chapter 3 Annotations and the runway are carried — designing operational signals

Out-of-coverage annotations **are carried through operators** (transport · 輸送). `shift(90, unit: day)` does not only move points by 90 days;
it **also maps the "unknown interval" 90 days ahead** — if the image of the unknown overlaps the evaluation range, an annotation
stands there.

**Run it and read** — the 2027 part of the contract data has not been obtained (a gap in the middle of covering). Evaluating the first half of 2028:

```kairos
# eval: 2028-01-01..2028-06-01
premise JP {
  calendar-system: Gregorian
  tz: "Asia/Tokyo"
  wkst: Mon
}

@JP
dues = [2026-03-01, 2026-09-01] covering: 2026..2026, 2028..2028
dues |> shift(90, unit: day)
```

```text
# 0 点（[2028-01-01, 2028-06-01) に該当なし）
# ⚠ 範囲外 2028-01-01..2028-03-31（dues covering 2026-01-01..2026-12-31, 2028-01-01..2028-12-31）
# 被覆サマリ
#   dues covering 2026-01-01..2026-12-31, 2028-01-01..2028-12-31 残走路 214 日
```

[▶ Open in the Playground](https://kairos-lang.org/en/playground/#s=cHJlbWlzZSBKUCB7CiAgY2FsZW5kYXItc3lzdGVtOiBHcmVnb3JpYW4KICB0ejogIkFzaWEvVG9reW8iCiAgd2tzdDogTW9uCn0KCkBKUApkdWVzID0gWzIwMjYtMDMtMDEsIDIwMjYtMDktMDFdIGNvdmVyaW5nOiAyMDI2Li4yMDI2LCAyMDI4Li4yMDI4CmR1ZXMgfD4gc2hpZnQoOTAsIHVuaXQ6IGRheSk&f=2028-01-01&t=2028-06-01)

Zero points, but the annotation says "firings in January–March **might have come from contracts in the second half of 2027 that have not been
obtained**". That no points come out, and that the reason none come out can be trusted, are **different things** — the latter is what the annotation carries.

The other operational signal is the **runway** ("残走路 N 日" in the coverage summary). The baseline is **from the end of the evaluation range** to
the end of covering — not from today. Negative means the evaluation has already passed the edge of the data. In practice,
feed it to threshold monitoring such as "warn when runway < 75 days", and prompt an update **before the data runs out** (graduating from
holiday-table operation that notices only after it ran out is what this one line means).

- Read first: [reference/shift](https://kairos-lang.org/en/reference/shift.html) (the note on transport)

## Chapter 4 Determinism — definitions that withstand a rolling horizon

The firing layer (発報層 — the execution system) does not evaluate a Kairos expression just once. It materializes **repeatedly while sliding the evaluation
window** (rolling horizon). The condition under which this operation holds is **determinism** — the same definition · **the same data**
(asof carries the version, and external is relative to the same snapshot) · the same range gives the same result, and
**the point sequence in the overlapping range matches even when the window moves** (§7.8. A result that changes when the data is updated is not a
break of determinism but an update of the supply).

**Run it and read** — every other Monday in two evaluation windows. In the overlap [1/15, 2/1) the only point in both is 1/19:

```kairos
# eval: 2026-01-01..2026-02-01
premise JP {
  calendar-system: Gregorian
  tz: "Asia/Tokyo"
  wkst: Mon
}

@JP
everyDay |> filter(d => weekday(d) == Mon) |> stride(2, from: 2026-01-05)
```

```text
2026-01-05
2026-01-19
```

What supports this is the design "**the phase belongs to the expression, not to the evaluation window**" — this is why the `from:` of stride is
required (counting from the start of the window would make the biweekly phase wobble on every evaluation).
The line of the division of labor is also drawn from determinism: **the language goes as far as enumerating a deterministic point sequence** · detecting firings missed while stopped
(missed-fire), delayed execution and idempotence are **the responsibility of the firing layer**. Because of determinism, the firing layer only has to remember
"how far it fired last time" to enumerate what was missed.

## Chapter 5 "Not yet" and "broken" — the supply boundary

Injecting data from an external system is `external` — the declaration "the content of this binding is resolved at run time", and the
resolver (解決子) is supplied by the implementation. Here the last distinction of operational semantics is needed:

- **Not yet** — resolution succeeded, and the content is empty (the same vessel of "legitimate empty" as the empty table in Chapter 1).
- **Broken** — resolution itself failed (**SupplyError**). It is **not** reinterpreted as empty.

**Run it and read** — evaluating external in an environment with no resolver does not silently become empty:

```kairos
# eval: 2026-01-01..2026-02-01
premise HRDB {
  calendar-system: Gregorian
  tz: "Asia/Tokyo"
  wkst: Mon
  source: "hr-db/holidays"
  holidays = external(kind: dates)
}

@HRDB
holidays
```

```text
供給エラー: 解決子がない——external HRDB.holidays（source: "hr-db/holidays"）は解決できない（ADR-46 判断 7 (a)）
```

Business-day calculation running on as "days with zero holidays" — that is the typical accident of if-statement systems, and Kairos
**stops it with the type**. "Not yet" continues evaluation with zero points (the runway prompts an update); "broken" makes the evaluation itself
a supply error (the firing layer **can choose** between degraded operation on the previous resolved value and stopping). For the same "no holidays
come out", the type of the output separates an empty you may continue with from a failure you must not — together with the three branches of Chapter 2,
this is the whole picture of "does not break silently".

## Closing — checklist for Grade 1

1. You can design covering as "a claim that does not touch the values" (including the weight of an open end. Chapter 1)
2. You can tell the three kinds of empty apart from the output and map them to operational branches (revisit · update · do nothing) (Chapter 2)
3. You can design the transport of annotations and the runway as monitoring signals (Chapter 3)
4. You can write definitions that withstand a rolling horizon (the phase belongs to the expression) and explain the line of the division of labor (Chapter 4)
5. You can distinguish "not yet" from "broken" by type and design operation during a supply failure (Chapter 5)

By now you should be able to read an operational meaning in **every line** of Kairos output (points · `# 0 点` · `# ⚠` · `# 被覆サマリ` · `警告:` · supply errors).
That is Grade 1 — the level not of someone who writes schedules but of **someone who designs systems whose schedules
do not betray them**.
